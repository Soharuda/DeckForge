The collection

Bundled card data, no connection needed

The card database travels with the app. Searching, building, checking a deck and playing a game all work with the phone in airplane mode; the network is for art and prices, not for the rules.

Binders you can trust

Every card you own lives in a binder, and importing one is a CSV - the export any scanner or marketplace already gives you. Cards you own twice count twice.

One card, one place

A card in a deck is in use, so other decks see the copy that is left rather than the copy you own. A deck that is short says how short, before you take it anywhere.

Printings are part of the card

Pick the set and the collector number you actually hold. The deck remembers it, the list shows it, and the table plays that print rather than the first one it finds.

Building

Typed the way you say it

"elendas hierophant" finds Elenda's Hierophant, "solring" finds Sol Ring and "dul" finds Lim-Dûl's Vault: the punctuation and the accents in a card name are not something the search asks for. Each result says how many copies the deck already holds, with a step either way, and every add or removal offers an Undo.

Synergy you can check

Suggestions come with their reasons: the oracle text that made the connection and the data behind the ranking. Nothing is a black box, so a suggestion can be argued with.

Legality per format

Commander's hundred singleton cards and its colour identity, sixty-card constructed and its copy rule: the deck is checked as it is built, not after it is finished.

Commander, properly

A general is chosen, the command zone is a real place, and the tax for casting it again is counted by the same rules the table uses.

The whole filter stack, in binders and the collection

Colour, type, rarity, set, mana, price, legal-in and what-it-does now answer in a binder and on the collection list, not just in search. The chip row counts how many lines match and one tap clears them. Sort direction is an arrow instead of A-Z.

Battle Mode leaves cleanly

Uninstall now asks Android's own package installer, the same one that puts the plugin on, so it can answer: removed, cancelled, or refused, in words. The tip jar also moved to the bottom of Settings.

A tip jar, if you want one

At the bottom of Settings there is a quiet support card: a dollar, three or five, or an amount you type, opening PayPal on your phone. Nothing is behind it. It unlocks no feature, and the app never asks twice.

Deleting a binder

A binder can be deleted from its options menu. When it holds cards the warning names the number and says they leave the collection with it, because the lines are the only record of them. A binder a deck builds from is refused: delete that deck instead.

New binders, made in the app

A binder no longer has to arrive with a CSV. New binder on the shelf names one, it appears straight away with nothing in it, and cards go in by Move or by building a deck from it. Two binders cannot share a name, whatever the case.

Rows or tiles, everywhere

Rows or pictures, wherever cards are browsed: the collection, a binder's contents, search and the want list. The choice is remembered between launches, and picked tiles show a border and a check so a selection is visible. How big the tiles are is a setting of its own, from compact to large. The decklist stays a list on purpose, because counts are read line by line while decks are built.

A loose pile that is easy to find

Cards a deck hands back go to Unsorted, which is held at the top of the binder shelf instead of being filed alphabetically. It is a normal binder otherwise: searchable, counted, and there for any deck to build from.

Editing a built deck moves real cards

Add a card you own to a built deck and the copies move out of their binder into the deck's. Take one out and its copies go back to Unsorted, where every other deck can reach them, instead of sitting in a binder no deck lists.

Updates that survive a dead connection

Settings, App checks the site and installs a newer build itself. A download cut off at ninety-eight percent keeps its bytes: the next try asks only for the rest, and says so.

Import forgiving of the list

A set with no collector number - "1 Angel's Grace (SOA)" - is read as a printing, not as part of the name, and a list that opens with a commander line files the rest of the deck as the deck instead of as ninety-nine more commanders.

Binder to deck in one step

Start from what you own and build outwards, or paste a list and be told what of it is already in the binders and what is not.

Buying what is missing

A cart, not a shopping spree

DeckForge only ever offers to buy the cards a deck is short, and it puts them in a cart first. Each line chooses its own printing - the one you want, or the cheapest that will do - and the total follows the choice, so the receipt is not a surprise. Removing a line puts the card back to the printing the deck asked for.

The table

Battle Mode is the other half

A deck is not finished until it has been played. Battle Mode is a second app that takes the deck you just built, prints and all, and puts it on a table against an opponent that plays by the engine's rules. Its own page is here.

Sharing

A link that is the deck

Share a deck as a link and it opens as the list, with prices and a way to hand it to the app. That is what the front of this site serves.

Installing

This is an Android app, installed from this page rather than from a store. Download the file, open it, and allow your browser to install apps if Android asks. It is signed with one key, and every release is signed with the same one, so an update installs over the previous version and keeps your binders.

Android 8.0 or newer. The download is about sixty-six megabytes because the card database is inside it.
