The table

One board, the whole screen

No menus to play from. Your hand fans along the bottom, ranks sit in perspective above it, and every question the engine asks is answered by touching the board itself.

Your piles where your hands are

Deck bottom left with the life total beside it, graveyard and command zone in the near corner, the opponent's piles in the far one. The phase rides in one pill, the stack in another.

Cards that look like cards

Printing-specific art from Scryfall, foils marked, tokens drawn as tokens. Images are cached, so a game after the first one needs no network at all.

Refusals in plain words

A play the engine will not take comes back with the engine's reason, not a guess. Nothing is pre-judged by the screen; the table decides and then says why.

Playing a turn

Lift a card to cast it

Drag a card out of the hand, or lift it and let go. Lands are played the same way. Targets, modes and X all ask on the board, in the order the spell wants them.

Tap abilities that actually work

Tap a permanent and the dock offers what the engine allows it to do right now, in the card's own words: a fetchland searches, shuffles and puts the land in tapped. Mana is still paid for you.

Combat you can see

Creatures that can attack breathe blue; the ones you have picked turn amber. Blocks are offered as pairs. First strike, double strike and deathtouch resolve the way they should.

End turn, or skip a step

One button ends the turn wherever the table is asking, and a second skips just this step: no attackers, no blocks, priority passed.

Decks and formats

It plays the deck in your binder

DeckForge hands Battle Mode the deck as built: main deck, commander, sideboard, printing, foil. Commander decks are played at 40 life with a general in the command zone that can be recast with its tax, and 60-card decks (Modern, casual) at 20. The opponent builds its own deck from the format of yours, so a commander deck meets a commander deck.

Four mulligans, chosen in settings

London keeps seven and puts one back per mulligan, Vancouver draws one fewer and scries, Paris draws one fewer, and Partial Paris sets aside any number and draws that many minus one. One free mulligan in all four, and the whole hand goes back in one decision rather than a card at a time.

Tokens are announced

The log now says when a token arrives - "turn 9: 3 Rabbit tokens arrive" - whether or not the engine's own narration is switched on. A spell that makes nothing says nothing, so the log answers the question either way. Settings and the log both close from an X in the top right.

A different opponent each game

Seven rivals instead of one: four commander decks (elf tribal, saprolings, big green, elf lords) and three sixty-card decks. One is picked at random for each game, and never the same one twice in a row.

Your printings

The deck is played in the printing it was built in. The set and collector number your decklist names are the ones the table uses, instead of whichever printing the engine happened to find first.

Settings before the deal

The deck preview has a Settings button next to Close, so the hand rule is chosen before the game rather than at the first hand: which mulligan, and which of the testing switches. The preview names the rule it will play, so nothing about the opening hand is a surprise.

Two testing switches

Settings has a Testing section. Infinite free mulligans deals seven every time with nothing to put back, so a hand worth testing with is one tap away. Table log puts the engine's own lines in the game log — a spell resolving, a token arriving — which is the difference between a card that is broken and a card whose work happened out of sight.

Rules from the source

The engine is a port of XMage's rules core: layers, replacement effects, cost payment, triggers, state-based actions. Cards the port has not finished are listed before a game starts.

Installing

Battle Mode is a separate app that DeckForge launches, so your deck crosses over without leaving the phone. Tap Battle mode in a deck and DeckForge checks this page, downloads the package, verifies its size, checksum and signing certificate, and installs it. Updates arrive the same way; your decks and settings stay put.

This copy is signed with DeckForge's own key, so a package from anywhere else will not install over it.
