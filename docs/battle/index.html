Battle Mode

Battle Mode is the separate app that plays a DeckForge deck with the XMage rules engine.

Cards that were never missing

0.22.0 fixes a refusal rather than a gap. The pre-game check named cards the engine cannot build, and some of those names were the check's fault. The sets register plenty of cards under the plain-letter spelling, while DeckForge sends the name as Scryfall prints it, marks and all, so "Hamato Ninpō" did not match "Hamato Ninpo" and a card the engine implements was reported as missing. Names are now compared with the accent taken off both sides, so "Lim-Dûl's Vault" and "Lim-Dul's Vault" are one card, and so are "Æther Vial" and "Aether Vial".

The pool itself was already whole and is still current: the sets are XMage's as of November 2026, 592 of them, 94,263 printings, none of which fail to build.

Upright to choose, sideways to play

0.22.0 puts the phone the right way up for each half of the job. The decklist is a column of cards, so the menu is portrait: the deck's name and its pictures at the top, the whole list down the middle, and Play under a thumb. Pressing it turns the phone and hands over to the table, which is sideways because two halves and a hand need the long side of the screen. Leaving a game turns it back.

The turn over is not a cut. The deck's cards riffle off the stack, the last one grows until it is the whole screen, and it crossfades into the table, which is already being dealt underneath it. The back of that card is drawn rather than downloaded, so the animation starts on the tap instead of waiting on a network.

The game moves too. Cards drop into their slots and the hand is dealt one card after another. A tapped permanent rotates and shrinks into its slot rather than jumping. Life counts to its new total and the disc knocks as it changes. A spell slides onto the stack, the phase rail lights the phase being played with a wash of brass, and every new turn announces itself. Everything the table is asking you to pick breathes, so a legal target can be found on a full board.

The table

The table is the screen Arena taught us to expect. It is landscape, and it is a table: their half mirrored above, yours below, creatures nearest the middle where combat happens and lands behind them, the graveyard and exile as piles on each player's plate, and your hand along the bottom edge with the cards you can actually play lit and the rest left quiet.

A card is played the way a card is played. Drag it out of your hand; while it is under your finger the card you would be playing is drawn at table size, and your own half lights up if it can be played there. Let go over your half and it is played, let go anywhere else and it slides back into your hand with a line at the bottom saying what is missing. Tapping a card no longer plays it, because tapping is how you read.

One tap on any card, in hand or on the table, puts the card itself up on the right at the size it is printed to be read, and a tap anywhere puts it away again. No dialog, no separate oracle panel: the printed text is the text.

The dock in the corner only appears when the engine wants something, and it asks in a sentence. Questions better answered by touching the table say so and get out of the way. Tapped permanents turn a quarter turn, creatures carry their power and toughness, tokens are marked, and the strip between the halves is the turn's phases, or the stack when something is on it.

Before the game

A deck used to be dealt the moment it arrived. Now there is a lobby. It checks the deck against the cards this phone can build, and while it does it says so: the name of the card it is on, how many of how many, and a bar that moves. Anything the engine cannot build is listed by name with a reason, instead of the deck quietly arriving with holes in it.

Then the decklist, in full, with the sideboard and the commander, and the number of pictures it will need. Pressing on asks for those pictures ahead of time: every card at its printing, the illustration as well as the whole card, the tokens the deck's own wording implies, and the other seat's deck, with progress on screen while it runs. The first game therefore starts with pictures already on disk rather than during the first turn.

One land a turn

0.16.0 fixes a bug worth naming. A land drop could be used twice in a turn, which is how a Mountain and an Exotic Orchard both appeared on turn one. The seat now asks the engine's own count of land drops as well as its own marker, and only plays a land the engine offered it in that main phase. A test plays an all-lands deck turn after turn and calls a second land directly, the way the phone did.

The pictures

Card art comes from Scryfall, once. Every card in the deck is resolved to its printing and its name, and both the whole card and its illustration are fetched while the game is being dealt, a few at a time and spaced out. They are written to a small index on disk with the pictures themselves, so the second game with the same deck asks the network for nothing at all.

Tokens are fetched ahead of time too, by reading the deck's own wording: a card that says it creates a 1/1 white Soldier makes "Soldier" into a warm-up request before the token exists. A token nobody predicted still gets its picture the moment it appears, and is remembered after that.

Nothing about a game depends on the network. Pictures arrive; the table plays either way.

What the table already does

More than one creature can block the same attacker, and the AI piles on by itself when that is the only way to kill something bigger than anything it owns. Instants are cast at instant speed. A deck that arrives with a commander is played as a commander game: forty life, the general in the command zone, and its tax tracked by the engine.

Download Battle Mode 0.22.0

Installing it from a browser means allowing your browser to install unknown apps. DeckForge can do the same thing itself, from the deck menu, and checks the download against this page's checksum and signing key before handing it to Android.

Both apps are signed with the same key on purpose: that is how DeckForge knows the app that answers com.soharuda.deckforge.battle.action.PLAY is ours and not an impostor.
